# Real data case studies

Dataset: 1,003 aligned daily adjusted-close observations from 3 January 2022 through 31 December 2025 for ten US-listed companies and SPY. Source provenance is stored beside the cache and identifies Yahoo Finance via `yfinance`. The default detector found 196 cooldown-separated company events at an absolute abnormal-return z-score threshold of 2.5.

## Case 1 GOOGL and AMZN

GOOGL–AMZN ranked first of 45 candidate pairs with SRCS 0.2352. The detector found 25 GOOGL events and 17 AMZN events. Event evidence contains both strong responses and near-zero responses, so the score reflects a recurring pattern rather than universal co-response. The pair shares broad technology and digital-advertising exposure, which is a plausible confounder. This result does not establish a direct commercial edge.

## Case 2 AAPL and MSFT

AAPL–MSFT ranked second with SRCS 0.2232. The event set contains 23 AAPL shocks and 24 MSFT shocks. Their large number of detected events gives the consistency term more observations than many pairs, but the score may still reflect shared mega-cap technology exposure, index membership or market-wide news left after a one-factor residualization.

## Ambiguous case GOOGL and NVDA

GOOGL–NVDA ranked last with SRCS 0.0192 despite 25 detected GOOGL events and 15 NVDA events. The low score shows that the method does not turn every pair of prominent technology companies into a strong candidate. It is an unsuccessful connection-recovery case in the sense that repeated sign-consistent delayed responses were weak under the configured five-day window. It cannot establish the absence of a real economic relationship; the window, daily sampling and event definition may miss one.

The corresponding time-series figures and event-level rows are generated by `python -m experiments.run_real_data`. All three cases remain descriptive statistical evidence rather than causal findings.
