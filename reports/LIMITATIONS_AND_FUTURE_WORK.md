# Limitations ethics and future work

## ML-specific limitations

- The classifier learns labels generated by the synthetic mechanism. High synthetic performance can reflect simulator fidelity rather than real economic validity.
- Pair rows within one graph are statistically dependent; seed-level splitting prevents direct leakage but does not make pairs independent observations.
- Real financial data lacks a complete, time-stamped ground-truth relationship network, so real ML probabilities are candidate rankings rather than verified accuracy.
- Domain randomization broadens training regimes but cannot reproduce every market confounder, structural break, volatility regime or reporting delay.
- Probability calibration is optimized for synthetic F1 and is not a literal probability of a supplier, ownership or contractual relationship.

- The synthetic network is static, binary and undirected.
- The propagation equation is linear and uses Gaussian noise.
- Daily returns can hide intraday ordering and mix simultaneous events.
- A single-factor market model leaves sector and macroeconomic confounding.
- Detected shocks are statistical extremes, not verified corporate announcements.
- Candidate rankings do not establish causality or a contractual relationship.
- Small sparse graphs make F1 sensitive to a few edge decisions.
- Hyperparameters were chosen for an interpretable prototype, not optimized for trading.

Future work should obtain independently sourced, point-in-time relationship labels, preserve their validity intervals, evaluate temporal transfer, quantify calibration error, and compare against sector/factor controls. Sequence encoders or graph neural networks are appropriate only after a substantially larger labelled corpus exists.
